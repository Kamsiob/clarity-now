# Clarity Now: Language Panel Review

A five round review of the phrasing corpora by specialists in behavior change, clinical psychology, linguistics, therapeutic communication and English prose.

**Nothing is implemented.** Part five contains the amendments awaiting approval.

**On the panel.** Five composite roles, not real people. I am playing each against a distinct evidence base. The research is real and sourced.

---

## The panel

**Dr. Elena K.** Behavioral scientist. Self-determination theory, autonomy support, message framing.
**Dr. Samuel A.** Clinical psychologist, motivational interviewing trainer.
**Dr. Wren M.** Linguist. Pragmatics, speech act theory, corpus linguistics.
**Odile B.** Therapist. Trauma-informed practice, burnout, digital wellbeing.

# ROUND ONE: FINDINGS

## Dr. Elena K., behavioral science

The observation corpora are sound. The guidance corpus has a structural problem and it is not a matter of taste.

<cite index="93-1">Research identified specific behaviors that support versus undermine autonomy. Autonomy-supportive communication takes the other person's perspective, offers choices within structure, provides rationales, and uses non-controlling language such as "you might consider" rather than "you should." Controlling communication relies on directives and phrases like "you have to" and "you must."</cite>

<cite index="100-1">Controlling message phrasing, meaning commands that do not provide choice, can thwart autonomy and lead to reactance, which is detrimental to the persuasiveness of the message.</cite>

Every line in the action bank is a bare imperative. *finish one thing already waiting in Work. spend ten minutes in Personal. decide whether Reading stays or goes to the archive.* Those are commands that do not provide choice. We wrote the controlling arm of the experiment.

The same study tested a second variable alongside language: <cite index="100-1">the provision of choice versus no choice.</cite> We offer neither. There is one plan and one accept tap.

## Dr. Samuel A., motivational interviewing

I want to name the thing this app has accidentally built.

<cite index="102-1">Miller and Rollnick call the "righting reflex" the powerful tendency among helping professionals to give advice, push recommendations and offer solutions. An ambivalent person instinctively reacts to advice by defending the status quo, reducing the likelihood of sustained behavioral change.</cite>

<cite index="107-1">Giving advice without permission is listed among MI-inconsistent responses, alongside confronting and arguing.</cite>

Layer six is a mechanized righting reflex. It observes a problem and issues a corrective. Every week. Forever.

But I am not arguing to remove it, because MI does not forbid advice. It forbids advice **without permission**. <cite index="108-1">The alternative offered is to reflect or summarize rather than direct.</cite> Our accept tap is a permission mechanism arriving after the advice instead of before it. That is fixable in the phrasing rather than the architecture.

The other thing I notice, and it is to the corpus's credit: the Pulse question format is already close to a good MI move. Stating a fact and asking the person to interpret it is genuinely reflective. <cite index="106-1">Reflective listening acknowledges without telling the person what to do.</cite> That part I would protect against any change.

## Dr. Wren M., linguistics

Four findings, in order of severity.

**One: agency attribution.** The observation corpus overwhelmingly uses transitive second person with the user as agent. *You added 9 things and finished 6. You changed what is active in Work three times.* Grammatically the user is the subject of every clause. In a context where the numbers are unflattering, sustained agentive framing reads as attribution of responsibility even when no evaluative word appears.

Compare *You added 9 things and finished 6* against *Nine things arrived, six left.* Identical facts. Different pragmatic force.

I am not proposing we eliminate second person, which would sound evasive and weaselly. I am proposing the corpus needs a **register axis it currently lacks**: the ability to shift to agentless construction when the content is unflattering, and to keep agentive construction when it is positive or neutral. Right now the choice is accidental.

**Two: dialect inconsistency.** The corpora are written in British English. `Reprioritising`, `prioritise`, `licence`, `behaviour`, `recognise`, `colour`. The developer has specified American. That is a global change and it reaches user-facing response labels, not just documentation.

**Three: the question format is doing more work than we credit.** Pulse asks *Deep work, or stuck?* That is a disjunctive question offering two complete alternatives. It presupposes that one of them is true. That presupposition is mostly benign here because both options are dignified, but there are cases where a third answer is the honest one, and the format forbids it.

**Four: hedging asymmetry.** Positive observations are stated flatly. *That is the most concentrated week you have had.* Negative observations are also stated flatly. *Personal is not on pause. It is stopped.* Symmetry sounds fair but it is not, because a flat negative lands harder than a flat positive. Something has to absorb that.

## Odile B., therapy

I read the whole corpus imagining a specific client: someone in the third month of burnout who downloaded a productivity app because they thought organizing would help.

Most of it is fine. Better than fine. The no-streak decision, the equal-validity responses, the willingness to say nothing at all. Somebody thought about this.

Three things worry me.

**`pt.gone.09`.** *"{areaName} is an area in name only at this point."* That is contemptuous. Not stern, not honest. Contemptuous. Read it as though it is about the area called Personal, in a week where the person could not manage Personal because they were barely managing anything.

**The escalation ladders assume the user is fine.** `persistence.s4.07` reads *"At some point an item stops being a task and becomes furniture."* That is a good sentence and it is written for someone who will find it wry. For someone stuck, it is the app noticing they have been stuck for a month and making a joke about it.

**Nothing in the corpus can hold a bad week.** The quiet-day family says a quiet day is valid, which is good. But there is no register anywhere in three thousand lines that acknowledges difficulty as difficulty. Every line is either neutral or gently pointed. There is nothing that simply says *that looks like it was hard.*

I am not asking for a mental health feature. I am asking for one register the corpus does not currently have.

## Harriet C., editor

The prose is better than most. Here is what I would send back.

**The list-of-three habit.** *You finished 6 things and added 9. Four focus sessions, 95 minutes, all in Work.* The three-part list appears constantly, particularly in the Report. It is a rhetorical reflex and once you see it you cannot stop seeing it.

**Sentence length is too uniform.** The Report body sits in a narrow band, roughly nine to fourteen words, for line after line. Good prose varies. A four word sentence after three long ones does more work than any adjective.

**"That is" is overused as a sentence opener.** I count it opening observations across at least six families. *That is more concentrated than your average week. That is the widest gap in March. That is your highest since June.*

**Some lines are trying too hard.** `ob.single.s2.l06`: *"{areaName} did not dominate the week. It was the week."* That construction, negate then restate more strongly, is a good move used once. It appears at least four times across the corpus.

**And a defect.** `hd.frag.06` contains a Cyrillic character. Already flagged, still present.

# ROUND TWO: DISAGREEMENT

## 2.1 Elena and Samuel versus Wren: how far to soften the imperatives

**Elena.** Every action becomes non-controlling. *You might consider ten minutes in Personal on Wednesday.*

**Wren.** That destroys the mechanism. The implementation intention literature is specific about form: <cite index="74-1">an implementation intention specifies the when, where and how of the behavior.</cite> Hedge it into an invitation and you have a goal intention with extra words, which is the arm that scored 32 percent.

**Elena.** Then we are choosing between two evidence bases.

**Wren.** No, because they are not in conflict at the point you think. The MI objection is to **unsolicited** advice. The II requirement is about the **form of a plan the person has adopted.** Those operate at different moments. Solve it with sequence, not with hedging.

**Samuel.** Say more.

**Wren.** The app offers a plan in a frame that is explicitly optional and grammatically non-imperative. The user accepts. On acceptance it is stored in first person as a proper if-then. The imperative never exists. The offer is nominal, the commitment is the user's own sentence.

> Offered: *One option for Wednesday morning: ten minutes in Personal before you open Work.*
> Stored on accept: *If it's Wednesday morning, I'll spend ten minutes in Personal before opening Work.*

**Samuel.** That resolves my objection completely. The permission arrives with the offer, and the directive form only ever exists as something the person said about themselves.

**Elena.** And it lets me add the choice variable. <cite index="100-1">The study tested provision of choice as a separate factor from language.</cite> Give the accept row two options rather than one: accept, and decline. An explicit decline is what makes the accept meaningful.

**Samuel.** Agreed, with the decline being genuinely costless and never mentioned again.

**Resolved.** L1, L2, L3.

## 2.2 Wren versus Harriet: agentless construction

**Wren.** Unflattering content shifts to agentless.

**Harriet.** Used carelessly that is the passive voice of a press release. *Mistakes were made. Nine items were added.* It sounds like the app is protecting someone.

**Wren.** Agreed, which is why it must be a controlled register rather than a rule. Not passive voice. **Intransitive or existential construction with the fact as subject.** *Nine things arrived, six left.* No passive, no agent deletion, just a different subject.

**Harriet.** That I can defend. *Nine things arrived* is a real sentence. *Nine things were added by you* is not.

**Odile.** And it should be tied to content, not to tone. The trigger is whether the fact is unflattering, not whether the line is trying to be gentle.

**Resolved.** L4.

## 2.3 Odile versus everyone: the difficulty register

**Odile.** One register that can acknowledge a hard week.

**Harriet.** The risk is sentimentality. This app's whole voice is that it does not perform sympathy.

**Odile.** I am not asking for sympathy. I am asking for accuracy. If someone has had four consecutive quiet weeks and their queues have doubled, *the pace has been dropping* is technically true and humanly incomplete.

**Samuel.** In MI terms she is describing a **complex reflection**. Not sympathy, not advice. Naming what is probably underneath the data.

**Elena.** With a firm constraint. It must never diagnose, never assume, and never ask about feelings. This app has no business inferring emotional states.

**Odile.** Then constrain it to observation about the pattern rather than the person. *Four quiet weeks in a row usually means something outside the app.* That claims nothing about them.

**Wren.** That works grammatically too. The subject is the pattern, not the person.

**Harriet.** With that constraint, and a hard cap on how often it fires, I withdraw the objection.

**Resolved.** L5.

## 2.4 The one that did not resolve: Pulse's binary

**Wren.** The disjunctive question forbids a third answer.

**Samuel.** It also does the work. A forced choice between two dignified options is what produces usable data and what makes the interaction one tap.

**Wren.** Add a dismiss.

**Samuel.** A dismiss is not a third answer, it is an exit. Different thing.

**Elena.** I would leave it. The quiet-day family already has three options where three are genuinely needed, which shows the format can flex when it must.

**Not resolved.** Recorded as L11, presented as a choice.

# ROUND FOUR: WHAT THE PANEL WOULD PROTECT

Recorded so nothing valuable is lost in the amendments.

**Samuel.** The Pulse format. Stating a fact and asking the person to interpret it is a genuine reflective move and I would not let anyone touch it.

**Odile.** The absence of streaks, and the fact that it is structural rather than a policy. And `letItBe`.

**Elena.** The silence floor. An app that can decline to speak has something almost nothing else in this category has.

**Wren.** The escalation ladders. Magnitude-keyed escalation with monotonic staging is a genuinely sophisticated piece of language engineering and it is the reason this will not feel scripted.

**Harriet.** The refusal to use exclamation marks, and the equal-validity test. Both are the kind of rule that only shows up in the absence of things.

# PART FIVE: AMENDMENTS

Numbered for approval. Nothing implemented.

## L1. Reframe the offered plan as an option, not an instruction

The rendered line becomes nominal rather than imperative. Cue and action are still combined, the if-then structure survives, but no bare imperative reaches the screen.

**Old frame:** `{cue}, {action}.` producing *On Wednesday morning, before you open Work, spend ten minutes in Personal.*

**New frame:** `One option for {cue}: {nominal action}.` producing *One option for Wednesday morning: ten minutes in Personal before you open Work.*

Additional offer frames, rotated:
```
frm.01  One option for {cue}: {action}.
frm.02  If you want one thing to aim at {cue}: {action}.
frm.03  {cue} might be the moment for {action}.
frm.04  Something to consider {cue}: {action}.
frm.05  A small one {cue}: {action}.
```

The action bank is rewritten from imperative to nominal. `finish one thing already waiting in Work` becomes `finishing one thing already waiting in Work`. Roughly fifty lines, mechanical but each one needs reading.

*Cost: moderate. Confidence: high. Unanimous.*

## L2. Store the accepted plan in first person

On accept, the plan is rewritten and stored as the user's own if-then, and that is the form shown on any later reference to it.

> *If it's Wednesday morning, I'll spend ten minutes in Personal before opening Work.*

The imperative form never exists anywhere in the app. This is what preserves the implementation intention mechanism while removing the righting reflex.

*Cost: low, a second render pass. Confidence: high. This is the resolution that unblocked the whole disagreement.*

## L3. Give the accept row an explicit decline

Two options, not one. `I'll do that` and `Not this week`. Declining is silent, costless, never counted, never referenced.

<cite index="100-1">Provision of choice was tested as a factor independent of message language.</cite> One button is not a choice.

*Cost: trivial. Confidence: high.*

## L4. Add an agentless register to the observation corpus

A fourth register alongside plain, observational and editorial: **`[N]` neutral-agent**, where the fact rather than the person is the grammatical subject.

Selection rule: when an observation's content is unflattering, meaning it names a decline, a neglect, an imbalance against the user, or an unmet expectation, prefer `[N]` if the family has one available. Otherwise proceed normally.

Examples of what is required:
```
ob.flow.s2.l01  [P]  You added 9 things and finished 6.
ob.flow.s2.l10  [N]  Nine things arrived. Six left.
ob.flow.s2.l11  [N]  The queues took on 9 and released 6.
ob.swi.l01      [P]  You swapped what was active 3 times this week.
ob.swi.l07      [N]  The front of Work changed 3 times.
```

**Constraint, and it matters.** This is not passive voice and it is not agent deletion. *Nine things were added by you* is banned. The construction is intransitive or existential with the fact as subject. Harriet holds veto on any line that reads as evasion.

Roughly forty new lines across the unflattering families.

*Cost: moderate. Confidence: high.*

## L5. Add a difficulty register, tightly constrained

One new family, `hardStretch`, firing on genuinely difficult patterns: three or more consecutive quiet weeks combined with growing queues, or a sustained decline across four weeks.

**Hard constraints, all mandatory:**
- The subject is always the pattern, never the person
- It never names or infers an emotional state
- It never asks a question
- It never offers help, resources, or advice
- It fires at most once every six weeks
- It cannot appear alongside a plan
- It cannot appear alongside `selfReportVsData`

Draft bench:
```
hard.01  Four quiet weeks in a row usually means something outside the app.
hard.02  Stretches like this are common and they are not usually about the app.
hard.03  A month like this generally has a reason that is not visible here.
hard.04  Some months hold more than they can move.
hard.05  This has been a long stretch.
hard.06  Nothing here has moved in a month. That happens.
hard.07  A period like this is not a failure of the system you set up.
hard.08  The app can only see what it can see, and it has not seen much.
```

*Cost: low. Confidence: medium-high. Odile's condition for withdrawing her broader objection. Elena and Harriet accept it only with every constraint above intact.*

## L6. Convert all corpora to American English

Affects user-facing response labels, not only documentation. Known instances:

- `switching.s1.r01` and `switching.s2.r01`: `Reprioritising` becomes `Reprioritizing`
- `colour` becomes `color` throughout, including the `Area color` sheet title
- `licence` becomes `license`, including `Open source licenses`
- `behaviour`, `recognise`, `prioritise`, `organise`, `apologise` and their derivatives take American forms
- `advertisement` and `adverts` become `ads`

A build test asserts no British form appears in any user-facing string.

*Cost: low, but it must be systematic. Confidence: certain.*

## L7. Break the list-of-three habit

The Report's `YOUR WEEK, HONESTLY` section currently tends to produce three parallel clauses. Add a composition rule: **no more than two parallel numeric clauses may appear consecutively.** Where a third would follow, the composer either drops it or re-realizes it as a separate sentence at a different length.

*Cost: low. Confidence: high.*

## L8. Vary sentence length deliberately

Add a **length axis** to Report leads: short (under 7 words), medium (7 to 14), long (15 to 24). The composer may not select two consecutive leads from the same band. This forces the rhythm Harriet is asking for without requiring anyone to write to a word count.

Roughly twenty new short leads are needed, since the corpus is currently thin at that end.

```
ob.flow.s3.l09  [P]  Six out, three in.
ob.quiet.l09    [N]  Two events. All week.
ob.single.s2.l11 [P] Ninety percent, one area.
ob.best.l07     [P]  A new high.
```

*Cost: moderate. Confidence: high.*

## L9. Cap the reused constructions

Two rhetorical moves are over-deployed and each is retained in exactly one place:

- **Negate then restate:** *X did not dominate the week. It was the week.* Retained in `ob.single.s2.l06` only. Three other instances deleted.
- **"At some point X stops being Y":** retained in `accumulation.s3.07` only. `ob.qp.l07` and one other deleted.

Add a catalogue test asserting no rhetorical construction appears in more than two families.

*Cost: low. Confidence: high.*

## L10. Reduce "That is" as an opener

Currently opens extensions across at least six families. Cap at two per report through composition, and rewrite roughly a dozen extensions to open differently.

*Cost: low. Confidence: medium-high. Harriet notes this is the smallest item on the list and the one most likely to be noticed subconsciously.*

## L11. The Pulse binary, unresolved

**Presented as a choice, not a recommendation.**

Keep the two-option forced choice as is, which produces clean data and a one-tap interaction, or add a universal third option along the lines of `Neither` or `Something else` which sacrifices data cleanliness for honesty.

Samuel and Elena favor keeping it. Wren favors the third option. Odile abstains, noting the quiet-day family already has three where three are needed.

*Panel split. Your call.*

## L12. Line-level fixes

Apply the round three table in full. Delete `pt.gone.09`. Fix `hd.frag.06`. Demote or delete `persistence.s4.07`. Delete the duplicated constructions.

*Cost: trivial. Confidence: high.*

**Dr. Wren M. dissents on L11.** He considers the two-option format a genuine pragmatic flaw rather than a design tradeoff, and wants it recorded that he thinks the panel is protecting a mechanism because it is elegant rather than because it is right.
